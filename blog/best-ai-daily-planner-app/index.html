Best AI daily planner app in 2026, compared

By one-by.one · Updated October 30, 2026

Motion is the best AI daily planner app if you want software to build your schedule and rebuild it when the day changes. Reclaim does similar work inside Google Calendar or Outlook and has a free plan, but no phone app. Pick Sunsama if you like planning each morning yourself, Tiimo for a visual day, and Claude or ChatGPT to try AI planning for free.

Motion and Reclaim: AI that builds your calendar

Motion is the most hands-off. Its AI prioritizes your tasks, places them on your calendar and moves them when plans change, with Outlook, Google and iCloud calendars in one view. Pro AI is $19 per user a month and Business AI $29, less if you pay yearly; there is a free trial but no free plan (pricing). Pick it if you want the schedule made for you, with no prompts to write.

Reclaim guards focus time, habits, buffers and meetings inside Google Calendar or Outlook. Lite is free for one user, with a one-week scheduling range. Starter is $10 a seat a month billed yearly, or $12 monthly, with a 14-day trial (pricing). There is no native iPhone or Android app; on the phone you work through your calendar. It is the better pick for meetings and teams.

Sunsama: a daily planning routine you run yourself

Sunsama walks you through the same steps every morning: pull in tasks, pick a workload you can finish, timebox it on your calendar, and close the day with a shutdown prompt (sunsama.com). It syncs with Google, Outlook and Apple calendars and runs on iOS, Android, Mac, Windows and Linux.

It costs $17 a month billed yearly or $22 monthly, with AI, MCP and Zapier included and a 14-day trial that needs no card (pricing). It is one of the pricier options, and the right one if you enjoy the planning itself.

Tiimo: a visual AI planner app built with ADHD in mind

On Tiimo you type or say what you need to do, and the AI breaks it into timed steps on a visual schedule. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Android, Mac and the web. Its FAQ says it was built with ADHD and autism in mind, for anyone to use.

The free version covers core planning on iOS and Android. Pro adds the web app and more, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan. Pick Tiimo if you would rather see your day than read it.

Todoist: a to-do list with AI helpers

Todoist is a task manager first; its AI tools turn speech into tasks (Ramble) and goals into steps (Task Assist), add Filter Assist and Email Assist, and include official connections for Claude and ChatGPT. It does not schedule your day. Beginner is free; Pro is $7 a month or $60 a year (pricing).

Pick it if you want one list you keep yourself. Our to-do app comparison covers its Claude connector.

Can ChatGPT or Claude be your AI day planner?

Yes, and both are free to start. Give one your fixed events, to-dos and habits, and it will put them in order, catch clashes and add the travel and prep you forgot. Mention a 10:00 meeting across town, laundry and a run you keep skipping, and it will put the wash before breakfast, have you leave at 9:30 and move the run to lunch.

Claude Pro is $20 a month, or $200 a year (pricing), and Claude for iOS can read your calendar, create events and add reminders on all plans.

The catch: a plan left as chat text does not track where you are. Scheduled tasks or reminders can ping you at set times, but nothing shows the next step as the day moves, or brings the laundry back when the 45-minute wash ends. Free AI daily planner apps

Claude and ChatGPT for planning in a chat, Reclaim Lite for the calendar, Tiimo’s free version and Todoist Beginner all cost nothing. Motion, Sunsama and one-by.one offer trials instead.

AI daily planner apps for iPhone

Motion, Sunsama, Tiimo, Todoist, Claude and ChatGPT all have iPhone apps; Reclaim does not. Most of them also run on Android. one-by.one is iPhone only and coming soon.

Where one-by.one fits

one-by.one does not plan. Your own AI does: Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant that supports MCP, connected at https://mcp.one-by.one/mcp. It fits habits between your events and to-dos, adds travel and prep, and saves the day to the app as cards. If you share calendars in the app, your AI sees the next seven days of the ones you choose; with Hide event names on, it sees “Busy” instead of the names.

The app never calls a model. It shows one card at a time, with Start, Done, Skip, Dismiss and Undo. Put the laundry on, and a 45-minute timer holds the wait and brings “Move laundry to the dryer” back when it ends. Records show what you did; Lock Screen and StandBy widgets show the current card. Nothing has to stay in your head.

It costs US$12.99 a month, or US$59.99 a year with a 14-day free trial for eligible new subscribers. It is iPhone only, in English, and not on the App Store yet. You need an AI you already use; the app gives you planning prompts and can copy one and open your AI. It does not auto-schedule your calendar like Motion or guide a morning ritual like Sunsama. Setup is on the Connect page, and the Claude steps are in adding a custom connector in Claude.

Which AI daily planner app to pick

  • Build and rebuild my calendar for me: Motion, or Reclaim if you want a free plan and can skip a phone app.
  • A planning routine I do each morning: Sunsama.
  • A day I can see, on many devices: Tiimo.
  • A list I keep myself: Todoist.
  • Try AI planning at no cost: Claude or ChatGPT.
  • My AI plans, and I want my phone to walk me through it: one-by.one, once it is out.

Many people mix them: plan in a chat, keep the list in Todoist, let Reclaim hold the calendar.
