Best AI daily planner app in 2026, compared

By one-by.one · Updated October 30, 2026

There is no single best AI daily planner app, because the apps do different jobs. Motion and Reclaim rebuild your calendar for you. Sunsama guides you through planning each day. Tiimo makes the day visual. Todoist keeps the list, and ChatGPT or Claude will plan a day in a chat for free. This guide checks each against its own site, gives prices and platforms, says what each does better than we do, and explains where one-by.one, our app, fits.

AI day planner app: what each one does best

Motion

Motion is the most automatic of the group. Its home page says an AI assistant schedules your day by prioritizing your tasks, placing them on your calendar and adjusting the plan when things change. It combines Outlook, Google and iCloud calendars in one view and has desktop and mobile apps.

Its pricing page lists Pro AI at $19 per user a month (7,500 AI credits) and Business AI at $29, with a discount for yearly billing. The page offers a free trial; confirm its length and renewal terms at signup. When we loaded the page it also showed “This plan is not available” next to the prices, so confirm the live price and your trial before you commit.

What Motion does better than us: it builds and rebuilds the schedule for you, inside your calendar, without you writing a prompt. We do not do that. Our app shows the plan; it does not make it.

Reclaim

Reclaim describes itself as an AI calendar that schedules work, meetings and life. Its pricing page lists Focus Time, Habits, Buffer Time and Smart Meetings on every plan, with Google Calendar and Outlook support. Lite is free for one user, with limits such as a one-week scheduling range and limited auto-rescheduling. Starter is $10 per seat a month billed yearly, or $12 monthly, and there is a 14-day trial.

One catch for phone users: Reclaim’s help center says it has no native iOS or Android apps today. You change things in the web app or in your calendar app.

What Reclaim does better: it defends your time inside the calendar you already use, and its free plan is real. It is also stronger for meetings and teams. If you do not need a phone app, it is a very good pick.

Sunsama

Sunsama calls itself a task manager, calendar and daily planner. Its site describes a daily routine: set goals, pull in tasks, choose a realistic workload, timebox on the calendar and finish with a shutdown prompt. It syncs with Google, Outlook and Apple calendars, and has apps for iOS, Android, Mac, Windows and Linux.

The pricing page shows $17 a month billed yearly or $22 monthly, with “AI + MCP + Zapier” included and a 14-day trial with no credit card. Its own FAQ on that page lists slightly higher figures, so check the current price at signup.

What Sunsama does better: the planning habit. It is built around a ritual you do yourself each morning, with a polished app on almost every device. It costs more than most apps here.

Tiimo

Tiimo calls itself a visual AI planner. On its site you type or speak your tasks, and the AI breaks them into steps, estimates how long each might take and arranges them into a schedule. It also has a focus timer, to-do lists, widgets and a web planner. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Android, Mac and the web, and its FAQ says you can import calendars from Google, iCloud, Outlook and others.

There is a free version with core planning tools, on iOS and Android only. Tiimo Pro adds the rest, including the web app. The 7-day trial is available only on the yearly plan. Tiimo lists no dollar prices; they depend on location and appear at checkout.

What Tiimo does better: making a day look like something you can see, on more devices than we will support, with a free tier. If you want a visual timeline and an Apple Watch, it is stronger than we are.

Todoist

Todoist is a task manager first. Its AI page lists Ramble (speech into structured tasks), Task Assist (goals into steps), Filter Assist and Email Assist, plus official connections for Claude and ChatGPT. It does not describe AI that schedules your day for you. On the pricing page Beginner is free; Pro is $7 a month, or $60 a year.

What Todoist does better: it holds everything, with recurring tasks, projects, sharing and a free tier. It is the best choice if you want a list you maintain. We cover its Claude connector in our to-do app comparison.

Can ChatGPT or Claude be your AI planner app?

For many people, yes. Give either one your fixed events, your to-dos and your habits, and it will put them in order, spot conflicts and add the travel and prep you forgot. Both have free plans. Claude’s pricing page lists Pro at $20 a month, or $17 a month billed as $200 up front, and Anthropic says Claude for iOS can read your calendar, create events and add reminders on all plans, under its iOS help page. We could not load OpenAI’s pricing pages in our check, so we leave out ChatGPT’s paid prices; see chatgpt.com/pricing.

The limit is what happens after the plan is written. A plan in a chat needs a way to follow through. ChatGPT can run scheduled tasks, and Claude can add events and reminders through iOS apps, depending on feature, platform and plan. Neither shows you the next step of the whole day as it goes, or notices that a wait has ended. We wrote two guides on this: how to use Claude to plan your day and a ChatGPT daily planner prompt, each with a prompt you can copy.

What chat does better: it costs nothing to start, it understands plain language about any kind of day, and you can change the rules in a sentence. No app on this list is as flexible.

AI daily planner app free: what you can use without paying

  • ChatGPT and Claude free plans. Planning in a chat costs nothing. Scheduled tasks and some connectors are limited by plan, so check yours.
  • Reclaim Lite. A free calendar-scheduling plan with the limits above.
  • Tiimo’s free version. Core planning tools on iPhone and Android.
  • Todoist Beginner. A free list with limited use of the AI features.

Motion, Sunsama and one-by.one have no free plan; they offer trials. one-by.one has no free tier at all. That is a real cost against the other apps here.

Is there an AI daily planner app for iPhone?

Yes, several. Motion, Sunsama, Tiimo and Todoist have iPhone apps, as do Claude and ChatGPT. Reclaim does not have a native one. Tiimo and Sunsama also cover Android, which one-by.one will not. one-by.one is iPhone only and not on the App Store yet.

AI planner app for ADHD: what the apps say about themselves

Tiimo is the one that speaks to this directly. Its site says it makes planning easier for ADHD’ers with visual timers, AI checklists and flexible scheduling, and its FAQ says it was built with ADHD and autism in mind while being meant for anyone. We make no claim about how any app affects ADHD, and one-by.one is not a medical or treatment product. If a visual, step-by-step day is what you want, look at Tiimo, and try the free version first.

Where one-by.one fits among AI day planner apps

one-by.one is different in one way: it does not plan. You bring your own AI, such as Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant that supports MCP, connected at https://mcp.one-by.one/mcp. Your AI fits your habits between your fixed events and to-dos and adds travel and prep, then saves the day to the app as cards. If you turn on calendar sharing in the app, it can see the calendars you choose; otherwise you tell it your events in the chat.

The iPhone app never calls a model. It shows one card at a time, with Start, Done, Skip, Dismiss and Undo. A timer holds a wait and brings the follow-up card back when it ends. Records show what you did, and Lock Screen and StandBy widgets show the current card. The idea behind it: your AI plans, you just do, so you do not carry the plan in your head.

The limits, stated plainly:

  • It is coming soon and not on the App Store yet.
  • It is iPhone only, and the app is English only.
  • It is a paid subscription with no free tier: US$12.99 a month, or US$59.99 a year with a 14-day free trial for eligible new subscribers.
  • It needs an AI you already use. The app gives you planning prompts and can copy one and open your AI, but you may need to paste it there.
  • It does not auto-schedule on your calendar like Motion or Reclaim, does not guide a morning ritual like Sunsama, and does not draw a visual timeline like Tiimo.

Connection steps are on the Connect page, and the Claude steps are in our guide to adding a custom connector in Claude.

Which AI daily planner app should you pick?

  • You want the schedule built and rebuilt for you in your calendar: Motion, or Reclaim if you want a free plan and can live without a phone app.
  • You like a deliberate daily planning routine: Sunsama.
  • You want a visual day, on several devices, with a free version: Tiimo.
  • You want a list you maintain: Todoist.
  • You want to try AI planning at no cost: ChatGPT or Claude. Start there either way.
  • You already plan with your own AI and want your phone to walk you through the result, one card at a time: one-by.one, once it is out.

These are not exclusive. Many people plan in a chat, keep their list in Todoist and keep their calendar in Motion or Reclaim. We will refresh this comparison as prices and plans change.
