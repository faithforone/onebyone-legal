Daily routine app for iPhone: what to look for

By one-by.one · Updated October 30, 2026

Pick a daily routine app for iPhone by how much your days change. If every morning looks the same, Routinery or Streaks will run it well. If meetings, school runs and errands move around, you need something that fits the routine into the calendar: Structured, Tiimo, or one-by.one, where your own AI does the fitting.

What should a daily routine app for iPhone do?

A list you tick every day works until a Tuesday with a 9:30 meeting, a school run and a dentist appointment at 4. The 20-minute walk that was “every day at 8” has nowhere to go, and by lunch the list is half undone.

  • It fits routines around the calendar. The walk lands in the gap after the dentist, or moves to tomorrow on a packed day.
  • It shows the next step without opening the app. Lock Screen widgets help most, plus StandBy for a phone charging by the bed.
  • It times steps and waits. Ten minutes of stretching is one kind of timer. A 45-minute laundry cycle is another: you wait, then you hang the clothes.
  • It lets you skip a step. Missing one thing should not sink the rest of the day.

Routinery

Routinery is built for predictable mornings: routines that run in the same order every time. On its App Store listing, each step “runs on its own timer while a voice calls out what comes next.” A widget on the Home Screen or Lock Screen starts a routine with one tap, per its features page.

It also connects to AI assistants. Its MCP page says an assistant can view, create and edit your routines after you confirm, and a separate Google Calendar connector can export routine schedules as recurring events.

Structured

Structured puts “all your tasks and to-dos into a single visual timeline.” Its App Store listing puts the drag-and-drop planner, focus timer, Live Activities and interactive widgets in the free tier. Structured Pro adds calendar and Reminders sync, recurring tasks and routines, and reschedules tasks you missed. It also has AI planning options.

It suits people who want meetings and habits on one line and like dragging blocks around themselves.

Tiimo

Tiimo is a visual planner with a timeline, a focus timer, widgets, Live Activities, tools for building routines and AI planning options. Its FAQ says you can import Google, iCloud and Outlook calendars. The 7-day trial applies only to the yearly plan.

A good pick if you like a colorful day you shape yourself, or need Android too.

Streaks

Streaks tracks up to 24 tasks a day. It has Home Screen widgets, counts timed tasks down on the Lock Screen, runs on Apple Watch and can tick a task off from Apple Health data. You buy it once; there is no subscription.

Enough if your routine is a handful of habits and a streak keeps you going.

Apple Reminders and Shortcuts

They are already on your phone. Reminders can alert you by time or place, hold subtasks and repeat. Shortcuts automations can run at a time of day, when you arrive somewhere or when you open an app. Together they make a rough routine, but you maintain every piece and nothing moves it around your calendar.

If you already live in Reminders, start there.

Free daily routine app for iPhone

Reminders and Shortcuts cost nothing. Structured’s free tier has the timeline, focus timer and widgets, but routines are Pro. Routinery’s free plan covers 2 routines with ads; Premium removes the limit and the ads. Tiimo has a free version. Streaks is a single purchase. one-by.one has no free tier; its yearly plan has a 14-day trial for eligible new subscribers.

Daily routine checklist app or daily schedule app?

A checklist app is for routines that repeat, like a morning routine or an evening wind-down. Order matters more than the clock. Routinery and Streaks lean this way.

A schedule app is for days that change: meetings move, the gym class is only on Wednesdays. The question is where the routine fits today. Structured and Tiimo lean this way. Plenty of people use both, a checklist for a fixed morning and a schedule for the rest.

A daily routine app that fits around your calendar

In one-by.one you do not arrange the routine, and the app does not either. Your own AI does: Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant that supports MCP, connected at https://mcp.one-by.one/mcp (setup on the Connect page). The app gives you planning prompts; tap one and it copies the prompt and opens your AI.

Tell it your habits, to-dos and fixed plans: gym three times a week, 20 minutes of reading, a 4 pm dentist on Tuesday. It fits the gym and reading between your events, adds the drive to the dentist, and saves the day to the app as cards. With calendar sharing on, it sees the next seven days of event titles and times from the calendars you choose. Turn on “Hide event names” and it sees “Busy” instead. one-by.one never writes to your calendars.

The iPhone app never calls an AI model; it runs the day one card at a time: Start, Done, Skip or Dismiss, and Undo for a mis-tap. A wait such as laundry becomes a timer, and when it ends the follow-up card comes back. Lock Screen and StandBy widgets show the current card, and Records show what you did. When the day changes, tell your AI and it updates the cards. For a longer walkthrough, see how to use Claude to plan your day.

The trade-offs:

  • You need an AI assistant of your own, and the plan is only as good as what you tell it.
  • It is iPhone only, with the app in English.
  • There is no free tier. In the US it is $59.99 a year with a 14-day free trial for eligible new subscribers, or $12.99 a month.
  • It is not on the App Store yet.

Which daily routine app should you pick?

  • The same routine every day, with timers: Routinery.
  • The day as a timeline you arrange yourself: Structured or Tiimo.
  • A few habits and a streak, paid once: Streaks.
  • Your own setup for nothing: Reminders and Shortcuts.
  • Days that change, with your AI placing the routine: one-by.one, once it is out.
