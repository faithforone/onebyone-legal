Daily routine app for iPhone: what to look for (2026)

By one-by.one · Updated October 30, 2026

A daily routine app for iPhone has to do three things: put your routines around the day you actually have, show the next step on your Lock Screen so you do not have to open anything, and time the steps that need timing. Routinery, Structured, Tiimo, Streaks and Apple’s own Reminders and Shortcuts each cover part of that. This guide says which part, checked against each product’s own pages on October 30, 2026, and where one-by.one fits.

What should a daily routine app for iPhone do?

Most routine apps start from the same idea: write a list, tick it each day. That works until a Tuesday arrives with a 9:30 meeting, a school run and a dentist appointment. A routine that ignores those becomes a list you fail by lunchtime. Here is what we would look for.

  • It fits routines around a real calendar. A 20-minute walk, stretching or reading should land in the gaps between events, and move or drop when the day is full. A fixed checklist cannot do that on its own. Either you re-order it each morning, or something else has to.
  • It shows the next step without opening the app. Look for Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, and ideally StandBy, the iPhone mode that turns a charging phone on its side into a bedside display. A routine you have to remember to open is a routine you skip.
  • It has timers for steps and waits. Ten minutes of stretching is one kind of timer. Laundry, an oven or a download is another: you wait, then something needs doing. Good apps handle both.
  • It lets you skip a step without breaking everything. Missing one thing should not turn the whole day red.
  • It is cheap enough to try. A free tier or a trial matters, because the only way to learn whether an app fits is to run a few real days.

No single app does all five well. Which one matters most depends on whether your days are mostly the same or mostly different.

Routinery

Routinery is the most direct answer to “routine app with timers.” Its App Store listing says each step “runs on its own timer while a voice calls out what comes next,” and its features page describes a widget for your home or Lock Screen that starts routines with one tap. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.

It also has an AI connection. Its MCP page says an assistant can view your routines, create new ones as drafts, edit steps and complete or skip tasks, always after you confirm. Connecting is free; for ChatGPT, follow OpenAI’s current custom MCP setup, which depends on your account and workspace. With a separate Google Calendar connector, the page says it can export routine schedules as recurring events.

On calendar sync, the pages disagree: a release note on the listing says calendar sync exists, while its FAQ says there is no direct sync with calendars. Check in the app before you rely on it.

What Routinery does better than us: it is out now, it has a mature step-by-step timer, it works on Apple Watch, and its routines run the same way every day. If your mornings are predictable, it is a strong choice.

Structured

Structured puts “all your tasks and to-dos into a single visual timeline.” Per its App Store listing, the free tier has the drag-and-drop day planner, a focus timer, Live Activities and interactive widgets. Calendar and Reminders sync, plus recurring tasks and routines, are listed under Structured Pro. It is on iPhone, the web and Apple Watch.

What Structured does better: a day laid out as a timeline is easy to read at a glance, and you can use it free. If you want to see meetings and habits on one line and drag things around yourself, it is a good fit.

Tiimo

Tiimo calls itself a visual planner built for real life, with a visual timeline, a focus timer, widgets, Live Activities and tools for building routines. Its FAQ says you can import calendars from Google, iCloud and Outlook. There is a free version on iOS and Android, and the same FAQ says the 7-day trial applies only to the yearly subscription. Tiimo does not publish fixed prices: they vary by location, so read the price at checkout.

What Tiimo does better: it is a polished visual planner with calendar import, available now, on more than one platform. If you want to build and adjust the day yourself and like a colorful timeline, it is worth a look.

Streaks

Streaks is a habit tracker and to-do list for Apple devices. It lets you track up to 24 tasks a day, has Home Screen widgets, counts down timed tasks on the Lock Screen, works on Apple Watch and can mark a task done from Apple Health data. It is a one-time purchase with no subscription; the page does not give a price. It does not mention a calendar.

What Streaks does better: it is the simplest, and you pay once. If your routine is the same few habits every day and you want a streak to protect, you may not need anything bigger.

Apple Reminders and Shortcuts

Already on your phone, and free. Apple’s Reminders guide says you can set alerts based on time and location, add subtasks, and choose Repeat under Date/Time. Shortcuts automations can run on a time of day, arrival at a location or opening an app. Together they can approximate a routine, but you build and maintain every piece, and nothing shapes the day around your calendar.

What Apple’s tools do better: they cost nothing, need no new account and work with everything else on your iPhone. If you already live in Reminders, start there.

App Store prices vary by country and plan, and some listings show several prices for the same plan, so we have not printed rival prices here. Check the price in your own App Store before you commit.

Daily routine checklist app or daily schedule app: which do you need?

These two searches describe different problems.

A checklist app is for routines that are the same every day: a morning routine, an evening wind-down, a few habits. The order matters more than the clock. Routinery and Streaks lean this way, and a checklist is easy to start.

A schedule app is for days that change. Meetings move, travel takes time, the gym class is only on Wednesdays. The question is not “did I do my routine?” but “where does it fit today?” Structured and Tiimo lean this way, with visual schedules and AI planning options; Structured Pro also reschedules missed tasks.

If you have a regular morning and a messy rest of the day, the honest answer can be both: a checklist for the morning and a schedule for the rest.

A daily routine app that fits around your calendar: where one-by.one fits

one-by.one takes a different route to the same problem. You do not arrange the routine, and the app does not either. Your own AI does: Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant that supports MCP, connected at https://mcp.one-by.one/mcp (see the Connect page). You tell it your habits, your to-dos and what is fixed. It fits the habits between your calendar events, adds travel and prep, and saves the day to the app as cards. If you turn on calendar sharing, it can see the next seven days of event titles and times from the calendars you choose. With “Hide event names” it sees “Busy” instead of names, and one-by.one never writes to your calendars.

The iPhone app never calls an AI model. It runs the day one card at a time: Start, Done, Skip or Dismiss, and Undo if you mis-tap. A wait, such as laundry or the oven, becomes a timer, and when it ends the follow-up card comes back. Lock Screen and StandBy widgets show the current card without opening the app, and Records show what you did. When the day changes, you tell your AI and it updates the cards. Which daily routine app should you pick?

  • The same routine every day, with timers: Routinery.
  • You want the day as a timeline and like to arrange it yourself: Structured or Tiimo.
  • A few habits and a streak, paid once: Streaks.
  • You like to build your own setup and pay nothing: Reminders and Shortcuts.
  • Your days change, and you want your AI to place the routine and the phone to walk you through it: one-by.one, once it is out.

These are not exclusive. Plenty of people keep a morning checklist in one app and let another run the rest of the day. Plans and prices change often, so check the sources below before you pay.
