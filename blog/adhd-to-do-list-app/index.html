ADHD to-do list app: what to look for, 5 to try

By one-by.one · Updated October 30, 2026

A good ADHD to-do list app shows one next step instead of the whole pile, puts time where you can see it, needs little setup and lets a missed day go without a wall of red. Tiimo and Structured are the visual planners, Todoist is the dependable free list, Goblin Tools unsticks a single task, and Things is the calm Apple-only choice. one-by.one, our app, takes another route: your own AI plans the day and your iPhone shows one card at a time.

Why long to-do lists stall people with ADHD

The NHS lists finding it hard to organise your time among the signs of ADHD in adults. CHADD’s article on time blindness suggests timers, visual schedules and breaking big tasks into smaller ones.

A long list leaves you to choose what comes first, with every item visible at once. Yesterday’s leftovers start to feel like a verdict. And a list never says how long anything will take.

What makes a to-do list app ADHD friendly

  1. One next step. A single thing to do now, or at least a Today view you can keep short.
  2. Time you can see. A visible countdown or timeline, so you do not track the clock yourself. Waits matter most, because that is where the thread gets lost.
  3. Low setup. A daily ritual of tagging and sorting becomes one more task. Fast capture beats a perfect system.
  4. Forgiving when you fall behind. You will miss days. Clearing what is left should take a tap, not an evening.

Most apps are strong on two of these. Pick by the one you need most.

Tiimo

Tiimo is a visual planner made with ADHD and autism in mind. The day is a color-coded timeline, the to-do list has a brain dump that turns loose thoughts into steps, and the focus timer counts down where you can see it. There are widgets and an Apple Watch app. Pro adds AI task breakdown, and its 7-day trial is on the yearly plan only. Pick it if you want to build the day yourself, in color.

Structured

Structured puts tasks, calendar events and routines on one timeline. The free version has the drag-and-drop timeline, an inbox, a focus timer and widgets. Per its App Store listing, Pro adds calendar sync, recurring tasks, AI day planning and Replan, which moves missed tasks to later. Replan is the most direct answer to falling behind of anything here, and Structured runs on Android as well as Apple devices.

Todoist

Todoist is a general task manager, not an ADHD tool, and a good home for everything. The free plan covers recurring tasks and up to five active personal projects in list or board layouts; paid plans add calendar layouts and time blocking. It is still a list, though, and keeping Today short is your job. It also connects to Claude and ChatGPT.

Goblin Tools

Goblin Tools is a set of small tools for when a task feels too big. Its best known one, Magic ToDo, takes “clean the kitchen” and breaks it into steps you can actually start. It is free on the web, with iOS and Android apps, and a low-cost Pro unlocks some features. The tools run on AI models, so leave private details out of what you paste. Keep it next to a planner for the moments one task will not start.

Things

Things is a paid, Apple-only task manager. Its Today list shows calendar events beside your to-dos, and checklists inside a to-do break it into steps. The Mac version has a free trial. If a quiet, well-made list is all you need, it is a pleasure to open.

Is there a free ADHD to-do list app?

Yes. Todoist is free for personal use, Structured and Tiimo have free versions, and Goblin Tools is free on the web. Start free, and pay only if you are still opening the app two weeks later.

ADHD planner app for iPhone: where one-by.one fits

one-by.one is our app. It is not a medical app and makes no claims about ADHD; it was built for a plainer problem: a plan is easy to write and hard to follow.

There is no list to scan. Your own AI plans the day: Claude, ChatGPT or another assistant that supports MCP, connected once on the Connect page. It fits habits between calendar events and to-dos and adds travel and prep. The iPhone app never calls an AI model itself. It runs the plan one card at a time, with Start, Done, Skip and Dismiss, and Undo for a mis-tap.

Say your morning is laundry, two emails and a 2 pm appointment. The phone shows one card: start the laundry. Tap Done and the 45-minute wait goes on a timer while the email card comes up. When it ends, moving the laundry to the dryer comes back as a card. Lock Screen and StandBy widgets show the current card without opening the app.

When the day slips, skip or dismiss what will not happen, mark what you already did in one catch-up step, and ask your AI to plan the rest.

The trade-offs: you need an AI assistant, it is iPhone only, the app is in English, and there is no free tier. It costs $59.99 a year with a 14-day free trial for eligible new subscribers, or $12.99 a month, and it is coming soon to the App Store. If you would rather type tasks in yourself, Todoist, Structured or Tiimo suit you better. Our guide to planning your day with Claude works with any app.

Which one to try first

  • You want to see the day as a timeline: Tiimo or Structured.
  • One big task will not start: Goblin Tools.
  • You want one free place for everything: Todoist.
  • You use only Apple devices and like a quiet list: Things.
  • You would rather your own AI plan the day and see only the next step: one-by.one, once it is out.

If you think you may have ADHD, a doctor can talk you through assessment and support. As for apps, try a few until one feels easy to open.
